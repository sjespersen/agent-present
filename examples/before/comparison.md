Here is a comparison of the three options for a local LLM box.

The Strix Halo system has 128 GB of unified memory, which is by far the most of the three, and it draws only around 120 W. Its generation speed is about 32 tokens per second, which is the slowest of the three, but it is also the cheapest at roughly €2,100 and it is fairly quiet.

The RTX 5090 is the fastest option at around 81 tokens per second, but it only has 32 GB of VRAM, which limits the size of the models you can run. It also draws about 575 W under load, costs around €3,400 for the card and system, and is quite loud.

The Mac Studio sits in between with 96 GB of memory and roughly 41 tokens per second. It is the quietest option — practically silent — but it is also the most expensive at about €4,600 and draws around 270 W.

Overall, I would recommend the Strix Halo. For this workload, being able to run large models matters more than raw speed, and it is the only option that fits a 70B model at 4-bit quantisation with enough room left for context. It is also the cheapest and most power-efficient option.
